The building at a glance 03

A temple to money, now open for culture.

Built in 1906–1908 by Mowbray & Uffinger and enlarged in 1931–32 by Halsey, McCormack & Helmer, who added Art Deco bronze and marble detailing to the rotunda. Behind a marble façade with columns and a dome, both the exterior and the interior are New York City landmarks, designated in 1994. Today the bank anchors a new development, and its interior has been restored.

Main level · Banking Hall, Mezzanine & Prism 04

One hall, nearly 18,000 square feet.

The banking hall is the heart of the building: a six-sided room of roughly 106 × 104 feet, organized around a ring of twelve columns at its center. A mezzanine looks over it, and the Prism next door extends the floor.
